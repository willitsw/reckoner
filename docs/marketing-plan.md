# Reckoner marketing and growth plan

Reckoner is a consumer app for reusable, nestable processes: hobby workflows,
pack-outs, setup routines, tune-ups, maintenance checks, and other repeatable
real-world steps.

## Positioning

### One-line pitch

> Reckoner helps serious hobbyists save and rerun the workflows they repeat:
> pack-outs, tune-ups, setup routines, maintenance checks, and project steps.

### Short pitch

> Reusable checklists for hobbies that have too many steps to remember.

### Problem

Hobbyists often repeat the same physical routines but keep them scattered across
memory, notes apps, YouTube videos, screenshots, spreadsheets, and paper lists.

### Reckoner's angle

Reckoner is not a generic task manager or corporate project-management tool. It
is for repeatable real-world processes:

- Pack my camera bag.
- Prep for a bike ride.
- Run 3D printer maintenance.
- Do sourdough bake day.
- Set up guitar practice.
- Get ready for a camping weekend.

The strongest differentiator is nestable live processes: one checklist can
include another, so "Pack camera bag" can include "Camera body check" and keep
that sub-routine up to date everywhere it is reused.

## Initial audience

Start narrow even though the product can serve many hobbies.

Initial wedge:

> Serious hobbyists with gear-heavy, repeatable routines.

Good first communities:

1. Photographers and videographers.
2. Cyclists and bike-maintenance hobbyists.
3. 3D printing hobbyists.
4. Camping and backpacking hobbyists.
5. Makers and workshop hobbyists.

Avoid launching as "checklists for everyone." Lead with concrete workflows that
already have repeated setup, packing, and maintenance steps.

## Messaging

### Main message

> Stop rebuilding the same checklist every time.

### Supporting messages

- Save your repeatable hobby routines once.
- Run them whenever you need them.
- Nest reusable sub-checklists inside bigger workflows.
- Keep setup, pack-out, and maintenance steps out of your head.
- Great for gear checks, hobby prep, and recurring projects.

### Terms to avoid early

- Productivity app.
- Task manager.
- Workflow platform.
- Knowledge management.

Those markets are crowded and too abstract for the first launch wedge.

## Landing page plan

### Hero

**Reusable checklists for serious hobbies.**

Pack-outs, tune-ups, setup routines, and maintenance checks you can run again
and again.

Primary call to action:

- Join the beta.
- Get early access.

### Example workflows

Show a few concrete examples instead of broad category claims:

- Camera bag pack-out.
- Bike tune-up.
- 3D printer maintenance.

### Differentiator

**Nest processes inside processes.**

Example: "Pack camera bag" includes "Camera body check," so shared sub-routines
stay up to date everywhere they are used.

### Email capture questions

Ask for:

- Email.
- Primary hobby.
- "What recurring checklist would you use Reckoner for?"

The free-form answer is product research as much as a signup field.

## Getting early users

### Lead with useful templates

Create public example templates or posts:

- Camera bag checklist for weekend shoots.
- Bike tune-up checklist before a long ride.
- 3D printer maintenance checklist.
- Camping pack-out checklist.
- Sourdough bake-day checklist.

End each post with:

> I am building Reckoner, an app for reusable hobby checklists like this. If you
> want early access, join here.

### Post where workflows already exist

Prioritize communities where people already ask what to pack, check, maintain,
or do before an activity:

- Hobby-specific Reddit communities.
- Photography, cycling, 3D printing, camping, and maker Discords.
- Facebook groups for hobby clubs.
- Local maker spaces.
- Photography clubs.
- Cycling clubs.
- Backpacking and camping forums.

Lead with the useful checklist or template, then mention the app. Avoid posting
"try my app" without a practical artifact.

### Ask for feedback

Good launch-post shape:

> I keep repeating the same prep and maintenance routines for my hobbies, so I
> am building Reckoner: reusable checklists for pack-outs, tune-ups, and setup
> workflows.
>
> I made this example bike tune-up process. What would you add or remove?
>
> If you want to try the beta, here is the signup.

This invites discussion instead of feeling purely promotional.

## Beta learning goals

Invite early users manually at first. Learn:

- What hobby are they using Reckoner for?
- What process did they create first?
- Did they run it more than once?
- Did they create nested processes?
- Where did they get stuck?
- Would they be disappointed if Reckoner disappeared?

The most important signal is not installs. It is whether someone creates a
process and comes back to run it again.

## Launch channels

Best channels when the product is ready enough:

1. Product Hunt for visibility.
2. Hobby-community posts for qualified users.
3. Indie Hackers or Hacker News "Show HN" with a builder story.
4. Short demo videos on TikTok, YouTube Shorts, Instagram Reels, or similar.
5. Niche newsletters for makers, hobbyists, and productivity-curious audiences.
6. Local clubs and maker spaces.

Suggested launch angle:

> I built an app for reusable hobby checklists because my real hobbies had too
> many recurring steps for a notes app.

Then show concrete examples.

## Content ideas

- What is in my camera bag checklist.
- The pre-ride bike check I stopped forgetting.
- A 3D printer maintenance routine you can run repeatedly.
- How to build reusable checklists for hobbies.
- Why I stopped using Notes for recurring hobby workflows.
- Nested checklists: the missing feature for real-world routines.

Each piece should point back to Reckoner.

## Growth loops to build toward

1. **Template sharing**: users can share a process template publicly.
2. **Community libraries**: curated sets such as "best camera bag processes" or
   "bike maintenance processes."
3. **Import from shared template**: someone sees a useful workflow and copies it
   into their Reckoner library.
4. **Creator attribution**: hobby experts can publish workflows with their name
   attached.

These loops can turn Reckoner from an app into a library of reusable hobby
processes.

## Early metrics

Track signals that show users understand and reuse the product:

- Landing page conversion rate.
- Email signups by hobby.
- First process created.
- First run opened.
- First run completed.
- Return usage: user runs a process again.
- Number of processes per active user.
- Number of nested includes created.
- Which template categories attract signups.

The core question:

> Which hobby group immediately understands Reckoner without explanation?

Double down on that group first.

## Recommended first move

Launch with one focused message:

> Reckoner is for reusable hobby checklists: pack-outs, setup routines, tune-ups,
> and maintenance workflows.

Test three niches with specific examples:

1. Photography: camera bag and shoot prep.
2. Cycling: pre-ride and tune-up.
3. 3D printing: maintenance and calibration.

Whichever group gives the clearest "I need this" response becomes the first real
marketing wedge.
